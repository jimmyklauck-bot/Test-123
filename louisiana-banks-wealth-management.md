# Louisiana Banks That Offer Wealth Management or Financial Advising

Compiled 2026-09-06. Scope: banks with a Louisiana branch presence that offer wealth management, trust services, private banking, or securities-based financial advising to Louisiana clients.

**How to read the platform column.** Most community banks do not run their own broker-dealer. They either operate an in-house trust department under the bank's own fiduciary powers, or they host a third-party broker-dealer program in their branches (LPL Financial, Cetera Investment Services, Raymond James Financial Services, Ameriprise Financial Institutions Group, Arkadios Capital, Infinex). That distinction is the useful one before a call: an LPL, Cetera, Raymond James or Ameriprise bank advisor buys on an open architecture platform, an in-house trust department is a discretionary fiduciary buyer, and a bank with neither is not an advisory prospect.

**Confidence.** High means the bank's own site or a broker-dealer press release confirms it. Moderate means a credible secondary source confirms the offering but not the platform. Low means the offering is implied and needs a call.

**Research constraint, read this before trusting a blank cell.** Direct site fetching was blocked in this environment, including FDIC BankFind, the Louisiana Office of Financial Institutions active bank directory, ICBA, and every individual bank website. Everything below was assembled by web search over those same sources. Two consequences: named advisors are sparse, and section 4 (the small community banks in the Capital Region, Northshore, River Parishes, and New Orleans metro) is the thinnest pass. A blank there means not found, not confirmed absent. Run BrokerCheck on any advisor before calling.

## 5. Consolidation notes, do not chase these

- **Progressive Bank** (Monroe/Winnsboro) was acquired by b1BANK in January 2026. Its Raymond James team, Progressive Financial Advisors LLC, now sits inside b1 Wealth Solutions Group.
- **Ouachita Independent Bank** (Monroe) merged into Cadence Bank in 2018, and Cadence is now Huntington.
- **Cross Keys Bank** merged with Planters Bank in March 2025.
- **First Bank & Trust** (New Orleans) was absorbed by BankPlus on 2022-03-01. Its FBT Investments business now sits under BankPlus Wealth at 909 Poydras.
- **St. Martin Bank & Trust** went into Home Bank in 2017. **Cameron State Bank** into IBERIABANK in 2011. **Tri-Parish Bank** (Eunice) into Pedestal, now b1BANK. **Citizens Bank of Ville Platte** is now an Investar branch. **Farmers-Merchants Bank & Trust** of Breaux Bridge is now a division of Merchants & Marine Bank of Pascagoula.
- **Argent Financial Group** in Ruston is worth knowing but is not a bank. It grew out of the Ruston State Bank trust department in 1990, and its flagship subsidiary Argent Trust Company is a national banking association that takes trust service agreements from banks that do not want to run their own trust department. If a Louisiana bank says it offers trust but has no trust officers, Argent is a likely explanation.

## 6. Where the ETF money actually is

Ranked by how usable each is for an ETF conversation.

1. **Open-architecture bank programs.** b1BANK and Woodforest on Raymond James; Home Bank, Red River Bank, First Federal, Synergy, Gulf Coast Bank of Abbeville, BankPlus and First Horizon on LPL; Hancock Whitney's branch program, Regions Investment Solutions and Bank of St. Francisville on Cetera; Community Bank of Louisiana on Ameriprise; Gulf Coast Bank & Trust on Arkadios; First Investment Services at First National Bank of Louisiana on Kestra. These advisors buy ETFs on the same platforms as any independent, and the program manager is usually the single point of entry for the whole bank. Citizens National Bank of Bossier City was previously listed here on Raymond James and has been removed pending the attribution check noted in section 1.
2. **In-house trust departments.** Hancock Whitney, Gulf Coast Bank & Trust, JD Bank, Washington State Bank, First American Bank & Trust, Renasant, and legacy Cadence now under Huntington. Discretionary fiduciary money, slower to move, larger tickets, and the decision usually sits with an investment committee rather than an individual advisor.
3. **Wirehouse and private bank offices with no Louisiana retail bank behind them.** Merrill in New Orleans, Baton Rouge and Shreveport; Wells Fargo Advisors in Baton Rouge, New Orleans and Shreveport; J.P. Morgan Private Bank in New Orleans and Baton Rouge. These are covered by their own home-office channels and behave like wirehouse offices, not bank programs.

Highest-value single follow-ups: the Hancock Whitney and Regions Louisiana financial-consultant rosters, which are longer than what surfaced here and are published on each bank's locator; and the b1BANK combined Raymond James program, which is newly consolidated at over $600MM and has three advisors.
